Poverty Is a Political Relationship

贫困是一种政治关系

Why the poorest are not outside the economy, but at its weakest edge

SDG 1 · 无贫穷 / No PovertyC1 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 100+6 min read

Poverty is not a residual of growth; it is produced and reproduced by institutions that distribute power and risk.

Beyond the income line

The international poverty line is a useful accounting device, but it tells us little about how poverty is produced. A household can cross the line and remain vulnerable to a single illness, drought or job loss. Multidimensional poverty—measured through health, education, housing and security—reveals what income alone conceals.

Poverty is relational. It describes a position in a set of institutions: land markets, labour contracts, tax systems, social protection and political representation. The poorest are not outside the economy; they are at its weakest edge, where risk is concentrated and voice is thinnest.

Social protection is not charity

Cash transfers, child benefits, pensions and health coverage are often framed as welfare. They are better understood as risk-sharing institutions. They allow households to invest, keep children in school and avoid selling assets during shocks.

Yet most of the world's poorest have no access to adequate social protection. Fiscal rules, debt service and tax exemptions constrain the very spending that would reduce poverty. The political choice is not whether the state intervenes; it always does—through tax, subsidies and legal rules that favour some groups over others.

The labour question

For most poor households, the main asset is labour. Poverty therefore depends on wages, working conditions and the terms on which labour is sold. Informal work, unpaid care and precarious contracts keep people poor even when they are employed.

A poverty agenda that ignores labour markets becomes a residual agenda: transfer payments compensate for low wages without changing the bargaining relationship that produces them. Minimum wages, collective bargaining, safe workplaces and the recognition of unpaid care are therefore anti-poverty policies.

From safety nets to power

SDG 1 promises to end poverty in all its forms. That ambition requires more than safety nets. It requires progressive taxation, universal social protection, investment in public services and legal empowerment for people living in poverty.

It also requires measuring poverty in ways that reveal inequality and vulnerability, not only averages. A country can reduce the headline poverty rate while leaving the poorest further behind. The question is not only how many people are above a line, but who has the power to move it.

中文译文 · CHINESE

贫困是一种政治关系

国际贫困线是有用的核算工具，却很少解释贫困如何被生产出来。一个家庭可以越过贫困线，却因一次疾病、干旱或失业重新陷入困境。多维贫困——从健康、教育、住房与安全衡量——揭示了收入所掩盖的东西。

贫困是关系性的：它描述的是土地市场、劳动合同、税收制度、社会保护与政治代表中的位置。最贫困者并非处于经济之外，而是处于经济最薄弱的边缘，风险集中、声音最弱。现金转移、儿童福利、养老金与医保常被说成福利，更准确地说，它们是风险分担制度，让家庭能够投资、让孩子留在学校、避免在冲击中变卖资产。但世界大多数最贫困者缺乏充分社会保护；财政规则、偿债与税收豁免压缩了本应减少贫困的支出。

对多数贫困家庭而言，主要资产是劳动力，因此贫困取决于工资、工作条件与劳动力出售的条件。非正规工作、无酬照护与不稳定合同让有工作的人仍然贫困。忽视劳动市场的减贫议程只是剩余议程：转移支付补偿低工资，却不改变产生低工资的议价关系。最低工资、集体谈判、安全 workplace 与承认无酬照护，都是反贫困政策。

SDG 1 承诺消除一切形式的贫困。这需要累进税制、全民社会保护、公共服务投资与对贫困者的法律赋权，也需要以揭示不平等与脆弱性的方式衡量贫困。问题不仅是多少人越过某条线，而是谁有权移动这条线。
