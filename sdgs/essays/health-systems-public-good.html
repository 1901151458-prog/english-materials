Health Systems Are a Public Good

卫生体系是公共品

Universal health coverage is not a cost centre; it is the infrastructure of a resilient society

SDG 3 · 良好健康与福祉 / Good Health and Well-beingC1 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 100+6 min read

Health systems are public goods: their value is measured not only in treatment, but in the collective security they provide.

The illusion of individual health

Health is often treated as a private outcome: individuals make choices, buy insurance and visit doctors. But health is produced collectively. Clean water, sanitation, safe workplaces, air quality, vaccination and epidemic surveillance are public goods whose benefits cannot be confined to paying customers.

The pandemic made this visible. A virus does not respect insurance status. The health of the most exposed worker shapes the risk of the entire community. Yet health systems were treated as a cost to be contained, not as infrastructure to be maintained.

Universal coverage as architecture

Universal health coverage means that everyone can access quality health services without financial hardship. It is not a single policy but an architecture: primary care, hospitals, medicines, health workers, financing and governance.

The world is far from this goal. Out-of-pocket payments push millions into poverty each year. Health worker shortages are concentrated in low-income countries, while wealthy countries recruit their staff. Medicines and vaccines are distributed by markets that reward purchasing power, not need.

Preparedness and antimicrobial resistance

Pandemic preparedness requires sustained investment in surveillance, laboratories, supply chains and public health institutions. It is a classic collective action problem: the benefits are invisible when prevention succeeds, so governments underinvest until a crisis arrives.

Antimicrobial resistance is a slower emergency. It is driven by the overuse of antibiotics in human medicine and agriculture, and by the absence of affordable diagnostics and new drugs. Addressing it requires coordination across health, agriculture, trade and finance—not only biomedical research.

Financing health as security

Health financing is a political choice. Countries can prioritise debt service, defence or tax cuts, or they can build a health system. The latter requires progressive taxation, public spending and international solidarity for the poorest countries.

SDG 3 promises healthy lives and well-being for all ages. Meeting it requires treating health as a public good and a security issue, not as a private market. A society that lets people die of preventable diseases because they cannot pay is neither just nor resilient.

中文译文 · CHINESE

卫生体系是公共品

健康常被当作私人结果：个人做选择、买保险、看医生。但健康是集体生产的。清洁饮水、卫生设施、安全 workplace、空气质量、疫苗接种与疫情监测都是公共品，其收益无法局限于付费顾客。疫情使这一点变得清楚：病毒不尊重保险身份；最暴露的劳动者的健康塑造整个社区的风险。然而卫生体系常被当作要控制的成本，而不是要维护的基础设施。

全民健康覆盖意味着每个人都能获得优质卫生服务而不陷入经济困难。它不是单一政策，而是一套架构：初级保健、医院、药品、卫生人力、融资与治理。世界距离这一目标还很远：自付费用每年把数百万人推入贫困；卫生人力短缺集中在低收入国家，而富裕国家招募其人员；药品与疫苗由奖励购买力而非需求的市场分配。

大流行病防范需要持续投资监测、实验室、供应链与公共卫生机构。它是典型的集体行动问题：预防成功时收益不可见，因此政府直到危机来临才投资。抗微生物药物耐药性是更慢的紧急状态，由人类医学与农业中抗生素过度使用、以及缺乏可负担诊断与新药驱动；应对它需要卫生、农业、贸易与财政的协调，而不仅是生物医学研究。

卫生融资是政治选择。国家可以优先偿债、国防或减税，也可以建设卫生体系。后者需要累进税、公共支出与对最贫困国家的国际团结。SDG 3 承诺各年龄段健康生活与福祉；实现它需要把健康当作公共品与安全问题，而不是私人市场。一个让人因付不起钱而死于可预防疾病的社会，既不公正，也不有韧性。
