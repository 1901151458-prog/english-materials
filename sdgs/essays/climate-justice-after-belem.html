Climate Justice after Belém

贝伦之后的气候正义

Mitigation without finance is coercion; adaptation without participation is disaster management

C1–C2 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 105+9 min readSDG 13 · SDG 10 · SDG 7

Mitigation without finance is coercion; adaptation without participation is disaster management.

The end of the safe operating space

In 2024, global average temperature exceeded 1.5°C above the pre-industrial baseline for the first time in a calendar year. The milestone does not mean the Paris Agreement has failed; it means the window for limiting overshoot is narrower, and the politics of adaptation and loss are becoming unavoidable.

Climate change is often framed as a collective action problem: everyone benefits from mitigation, but each actor has an incentive to free-ride. That framing is true but incomplete. Climate politics is also a distributive conflict over who reduces emissions, who pays for the transition and who is compensated for irreversible harm.

The countries that contributed least to cumulative emissions are often the most exposed. This is not a moral footnote; it is the central legitimacy problem of global climate governance.

Carbon inequality

Emissions are highly unequal within and between countries. The richest individuals and economies account for a disproportionate share of consumption-based emissions, while billions of people still lack access to reliable energy. A just transition cannot ask those with the smallest carbon footprints to bear the largest adjustment costs.

This is why “net zero” targets that rely on future technologies or offsets are politically suspect. They can postpone real reductions while shifting the burden to communities that have no capacity to adapt. Credible climate policy requires near-term cuts in high-emitting sectors, coupled with finance and technology transfer for developing countries.

The phase-out of fossil fuels is necessary, but it must be managed. Workers and communities dependent on fossil industries need alternative livelihoods, social protection and democratic participation in transition planning. Otherwise, climate policy becomes a recipe for backlash.

COP30 and the politics of implementation

The thirtieth Conference of the Parties, held in Belém in 2025, was symbolically important: it took the climate process to the Amazon, a region that is both a carbon sink and a frontline of deforestation and land conflict. It also exposed the gap between pledges and implementation.

The loss-and-damage fund, agreed at COP27 and operationalised in subsequent negotiations, is a recognition that some climate harms cannot be adapted to. But the fund remains small relative to need, and its governance is contested. Who decides which losses count? Are slow-onset events included? Can communities access funding directly, or must they go through national governments?

Adaptation finance is even more inadequate. The UN Environment Programme has estimated the annual adaptation finance gap in the hundreds of billions of dollars. Public adaptation spending is often counted as ODA, which means it competes with health and education. Private finance is reluctant to invest in public goods with diffuse returns.

Climate justice as institutional design

A just climate regime would rest on four principles. First, polluter responsibility: those with the greatest historical emissions should contribute the most to mitigation and finance. Second, capability: contributions should reflect ability to pay. Third, participation: affected communities should have voice in decisions that shape their futures. Fourth, accountability: commitments should be measurable, reportable and enforceable.

These principles point to concrete policies: progressive carbon pricing with compensation for low-income households; fossil-fuel subsidy reform; debt relief for climate-vulnerable countries; grants rather than loans for adaptation; and legal frameworks that protect environmental defenders.

They also point to a different role for international institutions. The World Bank and regional development banks should align all lending with the Paris Agreement, disclose climate risk and increase adaptation finance. The IMF should incorporate climate vulnerability into debt sustainability analysis. Trade rules should not constrain green industrial policy in developing countries.

Beyond the summit

The climate crisis is often described as a problem of political will. That phrase can be misleading: it suggests a single missing ingredient. In practice, political will is shaped by institutions, interests and narratives. It is easier to mobilise when costs and benefits are visible, when policies are fair, and when citizens believe that collective action can produce tangible improvements.

This is why climate justice is not only a moral demand but a condition of effective climate policy. Without trust, adaptation finance and transition policies will face resistance. Without participation, they may reproduce the inequalities they claim to address.

Belém was a reminder that the climate transition will be negotiated in places like the Amazon, not only in conference halls. The question is whether it will be a transition that protects the powerful or one that expands the possibilities of the many.

中文译文 · CHINESE

贝伦之后的气候正义

2024 年，全球平均气温首次在日历年尺度上超过工业化前 1.5°C。这个节点不意味着《巴黎协定》已经失败，而意味着限制超调的窗口更窄，适应与损失的政治正变得不可避免。气候变化常被描述为集体行动问题：人人受益于减排，但每个行为体都有搭便车激励。这一框架正确但不完整；气候政治同时也是分配冲突——谁减排、谁为转型买单、谁为不可逆损害获得补偿。

排放在国内与国家之间高度不平等。最富有的个人与经济体占消费排放的过大份额，而数十亿人仍缺乏可靠能源。公正转型不能要求碳足迹最小的人承担最大调整成本。“净零”目标若依赖未来技术或抵消，政治上可疑：它们可能推迟真实减排，把负担转嫁给没有适应能力的社区。可信的气候政策需要高排放部门的近期减排，并伴随对发展中国家的资金与技术转移。

2025 年在贝伦举行的第三十次缔约方会议具有象征意义：它把气候进程带到亚马逊，一个既是碳汇又是毁林与土地冲突前线的地区；它也暴露了承诺与落实之间的差距。COP27 同意设立的损失与损害基金，承认有些气候损害无法适应，但基金相对于需求仍然很小，治理也存在争议：谁决定哪些损失算数？是否包括缓发事件？社区能否直接获得资金，还是必须通过国家政府？适应资金更加不足；联合国环境规划署估计每年缺口达数千亿美元；公共适应支出常被计入 ODA，与卫生、教育争夺资源；私人资本不愿投资回报弥散的公共品。

公正的气候制度应建立在四项原则之上：污染者责任——历史排放最多者应承担最多减排与资金；能力——贡献应反映支付能力；参与——受影响社区应在塑造其未来的决策中有发言权；问责——承诺应可测量、可报告、可执行。这些原则指向具体政策：累进碳定价并对低收入家庭补偿；化石燃料补贴改革；气候脆弱国家债务减免；适应资金以赠款而非贷款为主；保护环境捍卫者的法律框架。它们也指向国际机构的角色：世界银行与区域开发银行应将全部贷款与《巴黎协定》对齐、披露气候风险、增加适应融资；IMF 应把气候脆弱性纳入债务可持续性分析；贸易规则不应限制发展中国家的绿色产业政策。

气候危机常被说成“政治意愿”问题。这个说法可能误导，仿佛只缺一种成分。实际上，政治意愿由制度、利益与叙事塑造：当成本与收益可见、政策公平、公民相信集体行动能带来改善时，它更容易形成。这就是为什么气候正义不仅是道德要求，也是有效气候政策的条件。没有信任，适应资金与转型政策会遇到抵抗；没有参与，它们可能复制其声称要解决的平等问题。贝伦提醒我们，气候转型将在亚马逊这样的地方谈判，而不只在会议厅里。问题在于：这将是一场保护强者的转型，还是一场扩大多数人可能性的转型？
