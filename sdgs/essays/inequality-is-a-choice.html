Inequality Is a Choice

不平等是一种选择

Markets produce inequality; institutions decide how much of it is tolerated

SDG 10 · 减少不平等 / Reduced InequalitiesC1 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 100+6 min read

Markets produce inequality; institutions decide how much of it is tolerated.

The many dimensions of inequality

Inequality is not only about income. It includes wealth, access to health and education, political voice, legal status and exposure to environmental harm. Inequalities intersect: gender, race, disability, migration status and geography compound one another.

Extreme inequality undermines growth, social trust and democracy. When a small group controls a large share of wealth and political influence, public policy tends to reflect their preferences. The result is a feedback loop: inequality shapes rules, and rules reproduce inequality.

Tax, labour and public goods

Progressive taxation is one of the most direct tools for reducing inequality. Yet many tax systems are regressive: they rely on consumption taxes and fail to tax wealth, capital gains and inheritances effectively.

Labour institutions matter too. Minimum wages, collective bargaining, overtime rules and social protection shape the distribution of income between labour and capital. Public goods—health, education, transport—reduce the value of private privilege.

Discrimination and recognition

Inequality is also produced through discrimination: in hiring, housing, credit and policing. Anti-discrimination law, affirmative measures and accessible public services can reduce these gaps, but they require enforcement and political commitment.

Recognition matters as much as redistribution. Groups that are excluded from decision-making are less likely to see their needs reflected in budgets and policies. Participation is not a substitute for redistribution, but it is a condition for it.

Global inequality and policy space

Inequality between countries remains large, and global rules shape it: trade, tax, debt and intellectual property. Developing countries need policy space to industrialise, regulate capital flows and protect social spending.

SDG 10 calls for reducing inequality within and among countries. That requires national policies and global reforms: fair tax cooperation, debt relief, labour standards and a development finance system that does not punish countries for investing in their people.

中文译文 · CHINESE

不平等是一种选择

不平等不只是收入，还包括财富、健康与教育机会、政治声音、法律地位与环境风险暴露。各种不平等相互交叉：性别、种族、残障、移民身份与地理彼此叠加。极端不平等削弱增长、社会信任与民主。当少数群体控制大量财富与政治影响力时，公共政策往往反映其偏好，形成反馈循环：不平等塑造规则，规则再生产不平等。

累进税是减少不平等最直接的工具之一。但许多税制是累退的：依赖消费税，未能有效对财富、资本利得与遗产征税。劳动制度同样重要：最低工资、集体谈判、加班规则与社会保护塑造劳资之间的收入分配。公共卫生、教育、交通等公共品降低私人特权的价值。

不平等也通过歧视生产：招聘、住房、信贷与警务。反歧视法、平权措施与可及的公共服务可以缩小差距，但需要执行与政治承诺。承认与再分配同样重要。被排除在决策之外的群体，其需求更难反映在预算与政策中。参与不能替代再分配，却是再分配的条件。

国家之间的不平等仍然巨大，全球规则塑造它：贸易、税收、债务与知识产权。发展中国家需要政策空间来工业化、监管资本流动、保护社会支出。SDG 10 要求减少国家内部与国家之间的不平等，这需要国家政策与全球改革：公平税收合作、债务减免、劳工标准，以及不惩罚投资于人民的国家的发展融资体系。

  • Recognition without redistribution is not enough—and redistribution without recognition can reproduce exclusion.
  • Global rules shape national inequality; policy space is a development issue.
