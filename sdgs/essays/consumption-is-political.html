Consumption Is a Political Act

消费是一种政治行为

SDG 12 is not only about individual choices; it is about the rules that shape what is available, affordable and disposable

SDG 12 · 负责任消费和生产 / Responsible Consumption and ProductionC1 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 100+6 min read

Consumption is shaped by systems, not only by individual virtue; SDG 12 requires changing the rules of production and disposal.

The myth of the sovereign consumer

Consumers are often imagined as free choosers. In reality, choices are structured by what is available, affordable, convenient and advertised. A person may want to buy sustainable products but cannot find them, cannot afford them or cannot verify their claims.

Consumption also reflects infrastructure: public transport, housing, energy systems and waste collection determine whether low-carbon choices are possible. Blaming individuals for systemic problems is both unfair and ineffective.

Supply chains and the circular economy

Most environmental impacts occur before a product reaches a consumer. Extraction, manufacturing and transport are often outsourced to countries with weaker environmental and labour standards.

A circular economy aims to design out waste, keep materials in use and regenerate natural systems. It requires product standards, repairability, extended producer responsibility and investment in recycling infrastructure. It also requires addressing planned obsolescence and the power of producers to shape demand.

Public procurement and the state as consumer

Governments are major consumers. Public procurement can create demand for sustainable products, from school meals to construction materials. Yet procurement rules often prioritise lowest price over lifecycle impact.

Greening procurement requires capacity, transparency and accountability. It can also support local producers and SMEs if designed inclusively, rather than creating barriers for smaller suppliers.

Consumption, inequality and sufficiency

The richest consumers account for a disproportionate share of emissions. Policies that focus only on poor consumers—such as regressive carbon taxes—can be unfair and politically unsustainable.

Sufficiency is not about depriving people of necessities; it is about reducing excess consumption while ensuring that everyone has enough. That requires public services, shorter working hours, repairable products and cultural change—not only individual guilt.

中文译文 · CHINESE

消费是一种政治行为

消费者常被想象为自由选择者。实际上，选择由可获得性、可负担性、便利性与广告塑造。一个人可能想买可持续产品，却找不到、买不起或无法核实其声明。消费也反映基础设施：公共交通、住房、能源系统与废弃物收集决定低碳选择是否可能。把系统问题归咎于个人，既不公平也无效。

多数环境影响发生在产品到达消费者之前。开采、制造与运输常外包给环境与劳工标准较弱的国家。循环经济旨在设计掉废弃物、保持材料使用、再生自然系统。它需要产品标准、可维修性、生产者延伸责任与回收基础设施投资，也需要应对计划性淘汰与生产者塑造需求的力量。

政府是重要消费者。公共采购可以为可持续产品创造需求，从学校餐食到建筑材料。但采购规则往往优先最低价格而非生命周期影响。绿色采购需要能力、透明度与问责；若设计包容，也可以支持本地生产者与中小企业，而不是为小供应商制造壁垒。

最富消费者占排放的过大份额。只针对贫困消费者的政策——如累退碳税——可能不公平且政治上不可持续。适度消费不是剥夺必需品，而是在确保人人充足的同时减少过度消费。这需要公共服务、更短工时、可维修产品与文化转变，而不只是个人负罪感。
