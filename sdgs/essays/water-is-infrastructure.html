Water Is Infrastructure

水就是基础设施

Scarcity is rarely about the total amount of rain; it is about who can store, move and pay for water

SDG 6 · 清洁饮水和卫生设施 / Clean Water and SanitationC1 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 100+6 min read

Water scarcity is rarely a natural fact; it is produced by the politics of storage, allocation and infrastructure.

Scarcity as a governance problem

Two billion people lack safely managed drinking water, and more lack basic sanitation. Yet the planet has enough freshwater for human needs. Scarcity emerges when water is polluted, poorly stored, unequally allocated or priced in ways that exclude the poor.

Infrastructure determines who gets water: dams, canals, pipes, treatment plants and storage. Governance determines who decides. A city can be “water scarce” while golf courses remain green; a region can flood while farmers lack irrigation.

Agriculture and the largest user

Agriculture consumes around seventy per cent of freshwater withdrawals. Improving irrigation efficiency, reducing water-intensive crops in dry regions and supporting farmers to adopt soil and water conservation can free up water for ecosystems and cities.

But efficiency gains can be captured by expansion: if farmers use less water per hectare and then irrigate more land, total demand rises. Water policy must therefore combine efficiency with caps on abstraction and protection of environmental flows.

Transboundary rivers and the politics of upstream

Many rivers cross borders. Upstream dams can reduce downstream flows, affecting fisheries, agriculture and drinking water. Cooperation requires data-sharing, legal frameworks and benefit-sharing, not only engineering.

Water treaties are often fragile because they were negotiated under different hydrological conditions. Climate change is making historical allocations obsolete. Flexible agreements, dispute resolution and basin-wide planning are essential for SDG 6.

Finance, pricing and the right to water

Water infrastructure is capital-intensive and long-lived. Public finance remains central, but many utilities are trapped in a cycle of low tariffs, poor service and political resistance to reform.

The answer is not simply to raise prices. Tariff design should protect a basic lifeline, recover costs for higher consumption and cross-subsidise the poor. Transparency, accountability and community participation are as important as investment.

中文译文 · CHINESE

水就是基础设施

约二十亿人缺乏安全管理的饮用水，更多人缺乏基本卫生设施。但地球有足够淡水满足人类需求。稀缺往往出现在水被污染、储存不足、分配不平等或以排斥穷人的方式定价时。基础设施决定谁得到水：水坝、渠道、管网、处理厂与蓄水设施；治理决定谁做决定。一个城市可以“缺水”，同时高尔夫球场保持碧绿；一个地区可以洪水泛滥，同时农民缺乏灌溉。

农业消耗约七成淡水取用量。提高灌溉效率、在干旱地区减少高耗水作物、支持农民采用水土保持，可以释放水给生态系统与城市。但效率收益可能被扩张吞噬：若农民每公顷用水减少，却灌溉更多土地，总需求会上升。因此水政策必须把效率与取水上限、环境流量保护结合起来。

许多河流跨越国界。上游水坝可能减少下游流量，影响渔业、农业与饮水。合作需要数据共享、法律框架与利益分享，而不只是工程。水条约往往脆弱，因为它们是在不同水文条件下谈判的；气候变化使历史分配过时。灵活协议、争端解决与全流域规划对 SDG 6 至关重要。

水基础设施资本密集、寿命长。公共财政仍居核心，但许多公用事业陷入低水价、差服务与改革政治阻力的循环。答案不是简单涨价；水价设计应保护基本生命线、对高消费回收成本、对穷人交叉补贴。透明度、问责与社区参与和投资同等重要。
