Food Security Is Geopolitics

粮食安全就是地缘政治

SDG 2 will not be met by food aid alone; it requires reordering trade, energy and conflict politics

C1 · 雅思 7.5+ / 托福 100+8 min readSDG 2 · SDG 12 · SDG 13

SDG 2 will not be met by food aid alone; it requires reordering trade, energy and conflict politics.

The end of cheap food

For much of the post-war period, food security was treated as a problem of production and distribution. The Green Revolution raised yields, and global food prices generally declined. That era is over. Climate shocks, conflict, energy price volatility and trade fragmentation have made food more expensive and less predictable.

The UN's State of Food Security and Nutrition reports that around 733 million people faced hunger in 2023, and that billions cannot afford a healthy diet. These numbers are not simply the result of bad harvests. They reflect the structure of a food system that is concentrated, financialised and highly dependent on fossil fuels.

Food is also a political instrument. Export restrictions, blockades and sanctions can turn a local shortage into a global crisis. The Black Sea grain negotiations illustrated both the importance of diplomatic arrangements and their fragility.

The fertiliser-energy-food nexus

Modern agriculture depends on synthetic fertiliser, which depends on natural gas. When energy prices spike, fertiliser prices follow, and farmers reduce application. Yields fall, food prices rise, and import-dependent countries face balance-of-payments stress.

This nexus means that food security cannot be separated from energy policy. A transition to renewable energy can reduce exposure to gas price shocks, but it requires investment in fertiliser alternatives, nutrient recycling and agroecological practices. It also requires trade rules that do not penalise developing countries for protecting their food systems.

At the same time, the solution is not simply to produce more food. Roughly a third of food produced is lost or wasted, and diets in wealthy countries are often high in emissions and low in nutritional value. SDG 12's call for responsible consumption is inseparable from SDG 2.

Conflict and hunger

Armed conflict is one of the strongest drivers of acute food insecurity. It destroys infrastructure, displaces farmers, disrupts markets and limits humanitarian access. The UN Security Council has recognised the link between conflict and famine, but enforcement remains weak.

Humanitarian food aid is essential in emergencies, but it cannot build long-term resilience. Food assistance that is procured locally can support local economies; cash transfers can preserve choice and dignity; social protection systems can respond faster than emergency appeals. Yet these mechanisms require predictable financing and political commitment.

The geopolitics of food also involves land. Large-scale land acquisitions, export-oriented agriculture and water-intensive crops can displace smallholders and undermine local food sovereignty. Responsible investment standards exist, but they are often voluntary.

Building resilience

First, governments should maintain strategic food reserves and regional cooperation arrangements. Reserves are not a return to price controls; they are a buffer against shocks and a tool for stabilising markets.

Second, trade policy should be transparent and predictable. Export restrictions can worsen global price spikes; international organisations should monitor them and provide a forum for coordination. Regional trade agreements can reduce dependence on distant suppliers.

Third, agricultural research and extension should prioritise climate-resilient, nutritious crops and agroecological practices. This is not a rejection of science; it is a demand for science that serves diverse farming systems rather than a narrow set of commodities.

Fourth, social protection must be strengthened. School meals, cash transfers and universal child benefits can prevent hunger from becoming a permanent crisis. They are also investments in human capital.

Beyond charity

The dominant narrative of food security is humanitarian: images of famine, appeals for donations, emergency airlifts. These responses are necessary, but they depoliticise the underlying causes. Hunger is not only a failure of production; it is a failure of entitlement, distribution and political accountability.

SDG 2 promises to end hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture. Meeting that promise requires treating food as a public good, not only a commodity, and recognising that the rules governing trade, energy and conflict are as important as the seeds farmers plant.

A world that produces enough food for everyone but allows hundreds of millions to go hungry is not facing a technical problem. It is facing a political one.

中文译文 · CHINESE

粮食安全就是地缘政治

战后很长时期，粮食安全被视为生产与分配问题。绿色革命提高产量，全球粮价总体下降。那个时代已经结束。气候冲击、冲突、能源价格波动与贸易碎片化，使粮食更贵、更不可预测。联合国《世界粮食安全和营养状况》报告指出，2023 年约有 7.33 亿人面临饥饿，数十亿人负担不起健康饮食。这些数字不只是歉收的结果，而反映了一个集中、金融化、高度依赖化石燃料的粮食体系。粮食也是政治工具：出口限制、封锁与制裁可以把局部短缺变成全球危机；黑海粮食谈判既显示外交安排的重要性，也显示其脆弱性。

现代农业依赖合成化肥，化肥依赖天然气。能源价格飙升时化肥价格跟随，农民减少施用，产量下降，粮价上涨，依赖进口的国家面临国际收支压力。这一关联意味着粮食安全无法与能源政策分离。向可再生能源转型可以降低对天然气价格冲击的暴露，但需要投资化肥替代、养分循环与农业生态实践，也需要贸易规则不惩罚发展中国家保护其粮食体系。与此同时，解决办法不只是生产更多粮食：约三分之一粮食被损失或浪费，富裕国家饮食往往高排放、低营养；SDG 12 的负责任消费与 SDG 2 不可分割。

武装冲突是急性粮食不安全最强的驱动之一。它摧毁基础设施、迫使农民流离失所、扰乱市场、限制人道准入。联合国安理会承认冲突与饥荒的联系，但执行仍然薄弱。人道粮食援助在紧急情况下必不可少，却不能建立长期韧性。本地采购可以支持地方经济，现金转移可以保留选择与尊严，社会保护体系可以比紧急呼吁更快响应；但这些机制需要可预测资金与政治承诺。粮食地缘政治也涉及土地：大规模土地 acquisition、出口导向农业与高耗水作物可能 displacing 小农、削弱地方粮食主权；负责任投资标准存在，但多为自愿。

建立韧性需要：维持战略粮食储备与区域合作安排；贸易政策透明可预测，监测出口限制，区域贸易协定降低对远距离供应方的依赖；农业研究与推广优先气候韧性、营养作物与农业生态实践；加强社会保护——学校供餐、现金转移、全民儿童福利可以防止饥饿变成长期危机，也是对人力资本的投资。

主流粮食安全叙事是人道主义的：饥荒图像、捐款呼吁、紧急空运。这些回应必要，却把深层原因去政治化。饥饿不仅是生产失败，也是权利、分配与政治问责的失败。SDG 2 承诺消除饥饿、实现粮食安全与改善营养、促进可持续农业。实现这一承诺需要把粮食视为公共品而非单纯商品，并承认贸易、能源与冲突规则和农民种下的种子同样重要。一个能生产足够粮食却让数亿人挨饿的世界，面对的不是技术问题，而是政治问题。
